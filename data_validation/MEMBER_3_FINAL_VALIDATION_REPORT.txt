--------------------------------------------------
3. PROJECT RELEVANCE
--------------------------------------------------

openFDA:
Useful as a machine-readable drug-label source for extracting and
validating medication safety information.

DailyMed:
Useful as a complementary SPL/label reference source for checking
drug-label information and supporting rule evidence.

Both sources can support:
- Drug interaction rules
- Contraindication rules
- Standard dosing information
- Renal impairment considerations
- Hepatic impairment considerations
- Warning and precaution information

--------------------------------------------------
4. IMPORTANT SAFETY LIMITATION
--------------------------------------------------

Label text must not be blindly converted into executable clinical
rules.

Safety rules should be:
1. Extracted from authoritative label evidence.
2. Structured into explicit rule fields.
3. Clinically reviewed/validated.
4. Versioned with their source.
5. Tested before being used by the Safety Resolution Engine.

The LLM should not independently interpret label text to make a
safety-critical medication decision.

--------------------------------------------------
5. DATA GAPS IDENTIFIED
--------------------------------------------------

The datasets provide useful label evidence but do not directly
provide a complete executable medication-safety rule database.

Additional rule-engine work is required for:
- Renal dose adjustment rules
- Hepatic dose adjustment rules
- Drug-drug interaction severity rules
- Contraindication rules
- Allergy-related rules
- Therapeutic duplication rules
- Dose/frequency constraints

The project should therefore treat these datasets as evidence and
reference sources rather than directly executable clinical rules.

--------------------------------------------------
6. FINAL VALIDATION CONCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------

openFDA and DailyMed are relevant and usable data sources for the
Autonomous Medication Safety Platform.

They contain substantial information related to dosing,
contraindications, warnings, drug interactions, and renal/hepatic
safety.

However, additional structured rule extraction, validation,
clinical review, and testing are required before the information
can be used by the autonomous Safety Resolution Engine.
